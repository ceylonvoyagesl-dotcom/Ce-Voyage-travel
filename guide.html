Weather & Seasons

Sri Lanka has two monsoons that arrive on opposite coasts, which means there is almost always a sunny coast somewhere on the island. Plan by region, not by calendar.

☀

December – April

The south and west coasts are at their best: Galle, Mirissa, Hiriketiya, Colombo. Dry days, calm seas, perfect for beaches and whale watching (November–April for whales off Mirissa).

☂

May – September

The south-west monsoon brings rain to the west and south, while the east coast shines: Trincomalee, Arugam Bay and the eastern beaches. Arugam Bay's surf season runs roughly April–October.

⛰

Hill Country

Ella, Nuwara Eliya and Kandy are green year-round, with the clearest views from December to March. Evenings are cool (10–18 °C in Nuwara Eliya) — pack a warm layer.

🐘

Cultural Triangle & Safaris

Sigiriya, Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa are driest from January to September. Yala's best wildlife viewing is February–July; Minneriya's elephant gathering peaks July–October.

Getting Around

Distances are short but roads are busy — allow generous time. The most memorable journeys are by rail; the most flexible way to explore is a private car with a local driver.

🚂

Train

One of the world's great rail journeys: Kandy → Nuwara Eliya → Ella. Book reserved seats (1st/2nd class) early through the official railway site. The coast line Colombo → Galle is also scenic.

🚗

Private car & driver

The most comfortable option for a full tour. Drivers handle parking, detours and local knowledge. Ce Voyage can arrange a driver for your whole itinerary — just ask us.

🚌

Bus

Cheap and frequent between towns, and an authentic local experience. Express buses on major routes are air-conditioned; local buses are basic but fun for short hops.

🛺

Tuk-tuk

Perfect for short trips in town. Agree the price before you start, or use the PickMe / Uber apps for metered fares in cities and tourist areas.

Culture & Etiquette

Sri Lankans are famously warm and welcoming. A few simple rules keep your visit respectful and smooth.

  • Temples: cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes and hats before entering. This applies to all Buddhist and Hindu temples.
  • Buddha images: never pose disrespectfully with Buddha statues or images — it is illegal to get Buddha tattoos or to wear clothing showing Buddha, and visitors have been refused entry or arrested.
  • Photography: ask before photographing people; avoid selfies with your back to a Buddha statue.
  • Hands & heads: use your right hand for giving and receiving; avoid touching people's heads, even children's.
  • Dress: modest dress outside beach areas — swimwear belongs on the beach, not in town or restaurants.
  • Greetings: "Ayubowan" with palms together is the traditional greeting — it means "may you live long".

Money & Budgets

The currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). ATMs are everywhere in towns and cities; cards work in hotels, restaurants and larger shops — keep cash for small vendors and tuk-tuks.

💵

Tipping

Not obligatory, but appreciated: 10% in restaurants if service is not included, LKR 200–500 per day for drivers, small amounts for guides and porters.

🏧

Cash & cards

Withdraw from bank ATMs (Bank of Ceylon, Commercial Bank) for fair fees. Notify your bank before travel, and carry some USD/EUR as backup.

Visas, Health & Safety

🛂

Visas & entry

Many nationalities (including France and most of Europe) currently enter visa-free or with a free ETA. Always check the latest rules on www.immigration.gov.lk before booking — and keep 6 months of passport validity.

💉

Health

Drink bottled water, use mosquito repellent (dengue risk is real), and protect against the strong sun. Travel insurance with medical cover is essential. Hospitals in Colombo and Kandy are good.

🛡

Safety

Sri Lanka is generally safe for travellers. Watch your belongings in busy markets, avoid unlicensed guides, and book transport through your hotel or a registered agency — like Ce Voyage.

🎒

What to pack

Light cotton clothes, a warm layer for the hills, a rain jacket, swimwear, reef-safe sunscreen, insect repellent, a universal adapter (Type D/G plugs) and temple-appropriate clothes.
